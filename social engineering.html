Understanding Social Engineering

Social engineering is the use of psychological manipulation to trick people into revealing information or taking actions that may put security at risk.

How Attackers Manipulate People

Instead of attacking a computer directly, an attacker may target the person using emotions such as:

  • Fear
  • Urgency
  • Curiosity
  • Trust
  • Authority
  • Excitement

Common Social Engineering Techniques

Phishing

Attackers send fraudulent messages that appear to come from trusted organizations or individuals.

Pretexting

An attacker creates a believable story or identity to convince someone to provide information.

Impersonation

An attacker pretends to be someone the victim trusts, such as a manager, bank employee, or technical support worker.

Quid Pro Quo

The attacker offers something in return for information or access.

Tailgating

Someone follows an authorized person into a restricted physical area without proper permission.

Scenario

You receive a phone call from someone claiming to be from your company's IT department.

They tell you that your account has a security problem and ask you to provide your password and verification code immediately.

They also warn that your account will be disabled if you do not cooperate.

What should you do?

  • Give them the information immediately.
  • Ask them to send you your password.
  • End the call and independently verify the request.

Security Tip: Never provide passwords or verification codes simply because someone claims to be from IT or another trusted organization.

Protect Yourself

  • Slow down when a request creates urgency.
  • Verify unusual requests independently.
  • Do not share passwords or verification codes.
  • Be careful with unexpected calls and messages.
  • Follow your organization's security procedures.
